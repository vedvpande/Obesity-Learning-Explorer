PRODUCT BRIEF
App name: Obesity Learning Explorer
The problem (2-3 sentences): The obesity epidemic is getting worse every year. Understanding the factors that relate to obesity (never predicts for a person) can help educate people about the condition. Students researching obesity for school struggle to find clear, trustworthy statistics. Official data sits in long reports and complicated tables, and quick searches often turn up numbers with no source. They need one simple place to look up, compare and understand obesity trends, and to cite them correctly.
Who it is for: (one specific user, e.g. "a busy parent managing a child's asthma
medicines"): Students trying to understand obesity, strictly for educational purposes. I.e, Priya, 15, a Year 10 student writing a health-class report on obesity. She needs her country's obesity rate, how it has changed since 1990, and a comparison with another country, all with a source she can cite.
Main features (3-6, must-have only):
Public: Anyone can access with website with no sign in required
Look up a country: pick a country from a dropdown to see its adult obesity rate, plus a "Copy this fact" button that copies a sentence with its source.
Trend chart: a line chart of that country's rate from 1990 to 2022.
Compare two countries: choose a second country and see both lines on the same chart.
Ranking table: all countries in a table you can sort by clicking the column headings.
Learn page: what an "obesity rate" measures, common causes, and how to cite the data, written in plain language.
Signed-in features: Accounts, logins or saving anything
My Report: signed-in students can save facts (country, year, rate, source) with a short note, then copy all their citations at once. This saves only facts and notes, never personal health information.
Database: one table for countries, one for yearly obesity rates (loaded from the real data file), and one for saved facts linked to the user.
Stripe (test mode only): a "Support this project" donation button, or an "Export citations" extra.
Analytics: track which countries are looked up most, with nothing personal.

Not included (out of scope):
BMI calculator, or any place to enter personal height, weight or health details
Diet, exercise or weight-loss advice
Live data from the internet (data is built into the app)
Maps (could be a stretch goal)
Children's or teenagers' obesity data, which is measured differently
Real WHO data on adult obesity rates: A built-in source dataset of yearly obesity rates (%) from 1990 to 2022 for every country. The numbers are modelled on the general shape of public WHO figures, and the page will say they are source data.
Health, privacy or safety risks and how I will handle them:
People misreading a number => every number shows what it measures (adults 18+), the year and the source.
Stigma or hurtful wording about weight => Use person-first language ("people with obesity"), neutral colours instead of red "danger" styling, and no before/after or body-shaming images.
Triggering people with eating disorders => No calorie targets, weight-loss goals or "ideal weight" numbers. The Learn page links to a support service.
Under-18 users => Google sign-in stores only name and email, each user sees only their own saved facts, and analytics never records personal or health information.
How I will know it works (success measure):
Accuracy: for 5 chosen country-and-year pairs, the number on screen and in the chart exactly matches the data file.
Usefulness: 3 student testers can, without help and in under 3 minutes, find one country's rate, compare it with a second country, and say whether rates went up or down since 1990.
Citable: Every copied fact includes the country, year, rate and source.
Works everywhere: it works on a phone and a laptop, in light and dark mode, with no errors in the browser console.
Privacy: Tested with two accounts, each user sees only their own saved facts, and analytics events contain no personal information.
